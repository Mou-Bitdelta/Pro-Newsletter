Market Commentary

 

Gold Tests a One-Year Low

 

Gold slipped below $3,500 per ounce on Wednesday, reaching its lowest level since September 2025. Stronger US economic growth and persistent inflation have reinforced expectations of further Federal Reserve rate increases, putting pressure on non-yielding assets.

 

Gold recovered towards $3,573 on Thursday as short-covering supported prices ahead of the US Thanksgiving holiday.

 

Bitcoin Attempts to Stabilise

 

Bitcoin recovered above $60,000 after testing the $57,000 region. The rebound comes amid continued uncertainty around monetary policy, institutional fund flows and broader risk appetite.

 

Equities Face Technology-Led Selling

 

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq recorded substantial weekly declines as investors reassessed AI-related valuations and technology-sector exposure. The sell-off highlights the sensitivity of growth-oriented equities to changes in interest-rate expectations.
02. MARKET INSIGHT
 

Gold Under Pressure as Rate Expectations Reshape the Outlook

 

Gold has experienced a sharp correction, falling below $3,500 per ounce for the first time since September 2025.

 

The decline follows stronger-than-expected US economic growth and persistent inflation, which have strengthened expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates in December.

 

Higher interest rates can increase the opportunity cost of holding gold, while a stronger US dollar can make the metal more expensive for buyers using other currencies.

 

The latest recovery towards $3,573 suggests some short-covering activity, although the broader market remains sensitive to upcoming US economic data.
 

What to watch

 
  • Federal Reserve expectations: Market pricing around the December policy meeting remains a key influence on gold.
  • US Treasury yields: Rising yields could continue to weigh on precious metals.
  • Dollar strength: Further appreciation in the dollar could add pressure to XAU/USD.
  • Geopolitical developments: Changes in global risk sentiment could influence safe-haven demand.
 

Key takeaway: Gold's near-term direction will depend heavily on inflation data, real yields and expectations for the Federal Reserve's next policy decision.

Event to Watch: US Nonfarm Payrolls

 

The November employment report, scheduled for 4 December, will provide another important indication of the health of the US labour market.

 

Stronger employment growth could reinforce expectations of tighter monetary policy, potentially supporting the US dollar and influencing gold, equities and other risk-sensitive assets.

 

A weaker report could ease expectations around further rate increases and influence market sentiment heading into the Federal Reserve's December meeting.
